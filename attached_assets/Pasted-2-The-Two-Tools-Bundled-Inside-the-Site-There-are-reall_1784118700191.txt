2. The Two Tools Bundled Inside the Site
There are really two connected mini-apps living under one homepage.
2.1 CC Rank Checker (the homepage tool)
You type in one or more domains (for example peec.ai, tesla.com, wikipedia.org) and get back, for each one: its Harmonic Centrality rank (called HC Rank), its PageRank, its subdomain count, a tier badge such as Elite, Top 1K or Top 100K, and a small line chart showing how both ranks moved across twelve historical snapshots stretching from 2023 to mid-2026. There is also a public Top 1000 leaderboard page listing the highest-ranked domains on the entire web graph, topped by infrastructure giants like googleapis.com, facebook.com and google.com.
2.2 Referring Domains Checker (the deeper tool)
This second tool, embedded from a companion Cloudflare Worker, is where the real analysis happens. Enter a single root domain and it returns an Authority Score and a Citation Score (both rescaled to an easy 0-100 range), a full list of every distinct domain that links to you, a bar chart showing how many of those referring domains are weak versus strong, and a breakdown of which top-level domains (.com, .io, .ai, and so on) send you the most links. It also draws a small radial network graph showing your site in the center connected to its top referring domains. A separate Link Gap Finder tab lets you compare your own domain against one or more competitors and returns every domain that links to a competitor but not to you, then flags the subset that is realistically attainable (linking to more than one competitor, mid-tier authority, not an impossible mega-site like Google or Wikipedia). A third tab, the Cheatsheet, gives plain-language tips such as prioritizing links from a wide variety of domains rather than many links from one site, and treating high-outbound-link domains as directories or guest-post opportunities rather than one-off link requests
3. The Core Idea: Two Different Ways of Scoring Importance
PageRank, invented by Google’s founders, treats every hyperlink as a vote. A link from a small, obscure blog counts as a small vote. A link from a domain that itself has thousands of trusted incoming links counts as a much bigger vote, because that domain is effectively lending you some of its own credibility. This is why one link from a well-known industry publication can matter more than fifty links from unknown directories.
Harmonic Centrality measures something subtly different: how short the average path is from every other domain on the web to your domain, following links. Picture the whole web as a subway map where a link is a rail line between two stations.
A domain with a low Harmonic Centrality rank (which the tool reports as a better, more central position) is like a station a few stops away from almost everywhere else in the city. It does not need one enormous vote of confidence; it just needs to sit in a well-connected part of the network. In practice, HC Rank rewards being widely and diversely linked, while PageRank rewards being linked by a few extremely powerful sites, and the two rankings frequently disagree slightly on the same domain.
3.1 Complete List of Every Logic and Algorithm the Tool Uses
The full document above explains the two headline scoring systems in depth. Here is every distinct piece of logic the tool runs, consolidated in one place:
Data foundation: uses Common Crawl’s free, public Host/Domain-level Web Graph instead of running its own crawler — three monthly releases merged into one graph.
Domain-level graph construction: collapses page-level links into domain-to-domain edges (about 121 million nodes, 3.9 billion edges), so it tracks referring domains, not raw URLs.
Harmonic Centrality algorithm: computes the average inverse shortest-path distance from every other domain to a given domain, rewarding domains that sit centrally in the link graph. Displayed as HC Rank / Authority Score.
PageRank algorithm: the classic recursive vote-weighting model, where links from highly-linked domains count more. Displayed as PageRank Rank / Citation Score.
Score normalization: raw graph-theory values (tiny floating point numbers) are converted into readable percentile ranks and 0-100 style scores.
Referring-domains counting: counts distinct linking domains rather than total backlinks, so one domain linking 500 times still counts once.
Outbound-domains logic: the same edge list read in reverse, used to build prospecting lists of domains a site already trusts enough to link out to.
TLD distribution logic: groups referring domains by top-level domain (.com, .ai, .io, etc.) to show link-profile composition at a glance.
Network graph rendering: a visualization layer plotting referring-domain relationships radially; uses the same edge data as the other metrics, just displayed differently.
Link Gap Finder (set-difference logic): computes domains that link to one or more competitor domains but not to your domain, producing a raw gap list.
Relevance/attainability filter: applies heuristics on top of the raw gap list to flag a smaller subset as niche-relevant and realistically attainable.
Platform-opportunity detection: separately flags very-high-outbound-link domains (like github.io) as open platforms worth listing on, versus one-off editorial link targets.
Serving architecture: pre-computed results only (nothing scraped live) - Cloudflare KV plus a Cloudflare Worker for instant CC Rank Checker lookups, and a thin Worker proxying a Hugging Face Space backend for the heavier Referring Domains / Link Gap logic.
Public API layer: both tools expose plain JSON endpoints (/api/report and /api/gap) behind the scenes, so the same logic can be scripted or embedded without the front-end.
Freshness/update-cadence logic: tied to Common Crawl’s own release schedule rather than continuous crawling, so brand-new pages or links can take time to appear.
